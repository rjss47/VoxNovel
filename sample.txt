Sarah: Elias? Are you actually up here? It smells like a hundred years of damp newspapers and forgotten resolutions.

Elias: Careful where you step, Sarah. The floorboards near the window are more suggestion than substance at this point. And for your information, it’s not damp newspapers—it’s "patina."

Sarah: Patina doesn't make me sneeze like this. Why are you sitting in the dark? You’ve been up here for three hours. Everyone downstairs thinks you’ve finally been claimed by the ghost of Great Aunt Martha.

Elias: I found it. The box we were looking for. It was tucked behind that stack of old encyclopedias—the ones from 1984 that claim Pluto is still a definitive planet and the internet is a passing fad.

Sarah: No way. You actually found the Polaroids? I thought they were lost when the basement flooded in '09.

Elias: Look at this one. It’s the summer we tried to build that raft. You remember? We thought we could sail all the way to the coast using nothing but a bedsheet and sheer teenage confidence.

Sarah: Oh, wow. Look at my hair! Why did nobody tell me that neon headbands weren't a good look? I look like a highlighter that’s had a very bad day. And you... you look remarkably the same, just with fewer wrinkles and a much more optimistic expression.

Elias: I remember that day vividly. The water was freezing. We didn't even make it ten feet before the "S.S. Unsinkable" lived up to its name in the most ironic way possible. 

Sarah: We were soaking wet and shivering, but I don't think I’ve ever laughed harder. (Pause) Do you ever miss it? That specific kind of fearlessness?

Elias: Sometimes. But then I remember that my knees hurt when it rains now, and I realize I’m perfectly happy being "fearful" on solid ground with a cup of tea.

Sarah: Fair point. Come on, bring the box downstairs. The light is better, and Mom is threatening to start the slideshow without us.

Elias: Just give me a second. I want to see if I can find the one of the dog in the tuxedo. 

Sarah: If you find that, I’m framing it. Now move it, Elias! The "patina" is starting to get on my nerves.
